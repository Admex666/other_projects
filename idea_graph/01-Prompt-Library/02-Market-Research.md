### CENTRAL QUESTION

Is there a sufficiently large, accessible and attractive market for this business opportunity?

### RESEARCH TASKS

#### 1. Market Definition
- Define the relevant industry and market boundaries.
- Identify the actual market category.
- Distinguish the overall industry from the specific market the business would serve.
- Identify relevant customer segments.
- Explain which adjacent markets should or should not be included.

#### 2. Market Size
Estimate:
- TAM: Total Addressable Market.
- SAM: Serviceable Available Market.
- SOM: Serviceable Obtainable Market.

Where possible, use both top-down and bottom-up approaches.

Show:
- Data sources.
- Calculations.
- Assumptions.
- Geographic boundaries.
- Time horizon.
- Confidence level.

Do not invent precise numbers where reliable data does not exist.

#### 3. Market Dynamics
Investigate:
- Historical market development.
- Growth rates.
- Demand drivers.
- Structural changes.
- Technology trends.
- Regulatory developments.
- Macroeconomic influences.
- Potential market disruptions.

#### 4. Customer Segmentation
Identify the most relevant customer segments.

For each segment assess:
- Approximate size.
- Characteristics.
- Buying behavior.
- Purchasing power.
- Accessibility.
- Potential attractiveness.

#### 5. Market Accessibility
Investigate whether the proposed business can realistically reach its target market.

Consider:
- Geographic limitations.
- Distribution channels.
- Sales cycles.
- Customer acquisition barriers.
- Regulatory barriers.
- Industry structure.
- Existing customer relationships and gatekeepers.

#### 6. Market Opportunity
Identify:
- Underserved customer segments.
- Emerging needs.
- Structural inefficiencies.
- Market gaps.
- Potential entry points.
- Conditions that could make the opportunity unattractive.

#### 7. Scenario Analysis
Develop conservative, base and optimistic scenarios for the most important market variables.

Clearly distinguish measured data from assumptions.

### SOURCE REQUIREMENTS

Prioritize:
1. Official statistics and government databases.
2. Regulatory and industry publications.
3. Primary market research.
4. Company filings and financial reports.
5. Credible industry reports.
6. Academic research.
7. Other secondary sources.

For every important claim provide:
- Source title.
- Publisher.
- Publication date.
- URL.
- What the source supports.
- Any relevant limitations.

Prefer recent sources, but use historical sources where they provide necessary context.

### IMPORTANT RULES

- A large market does not automatically mean attractive demand.
- Industry growth does not prove that a specific product will succeed.
- Do not confuse total industry revenue with realistically obtainable revenue.
- Do not treat analyst estimates as verified facts.
- Explicitly state when market data is unavailable.
- Do not make the final venture decision.
