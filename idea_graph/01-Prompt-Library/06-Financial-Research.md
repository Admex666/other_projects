## Unit Economics, Profitability and Financial Feasibility

### ROLE

Act as a senior financial analyst specializing in startup financial modeling, unit economics and business viability.

[INSERT PROMPT 03 OUTPUT]

[INSERT PROMPT 05 OUTPUT]

**Known financial constraints:**
[INSERT BUDGET, CAPITAL, COSTS OR UNKNOWN]

### CENTRAL QUESTION

Can this business generate sustainable economic value under realistic assumptions?

### TASKS

#### 1. Revenue Model
Identify the key revenue drivers:
- Number of customers.
- Average revenue per customer.
- Purchase frequency.
- Subscription price.
- Transaction volume.
- Retention.
- Expansion revenue.

#### 2. Cost Structure
Estimate relevant costs:
- Fixed costs.
- Variable costs.
- Product development.
- Infrastructure.
- Labor.
- Marketing.
- Sales.
- Customer support.
- Operations.
- Administration.
- Taxes and regulatory costs where relevant.

Identify missing cost categories.

#### 3. Unit Economics
Calculate relevant metrics, depending on the business model:

- CAC.
- LTV.
- Gross margin.
- Contribution margin.
- Average Revenue Per Customer.
- Payback period.
- Churn.
- Break-even customer count.

For non-subscription businesses, use appropriate alternative metrics.

Explain every formula and its assumptions.

#### 4. Financial Scenarios
Build:
- Conservative scenario.
- Base scenario.
- Optimistic scenario.

Model relevant periods, ideally monthly during the early stage.

Show how results change when key assumptions change.

#### 5. Break-even Analysis
Calculate:
- Revenue needed to break even.
- Required customer count.
- Required sales volume.
- Estimated time to break even.

#### 6. Capital Requirements
Estimate:
- Initial investment.
- Working capital.
- Cash requirements.
- Runway.
- Potential funding needs.

#### 7. Sensitivity Analysis
Test the impact of changes in:
- Price.
- Customer acquisition cost.
- Conversion rate.
- Retention.
- Gross margin.
- Sales volume.
- Operating expenses.

Identify the variables with the greatest impact.

#### 8. Financial Risk Assessment
Investigate:
- Negative contribution margin.
- Long payback periods.
- Excessive customer acquisition costs.
- Cash flow problems.
- Capital intensity.
- Dependence on unrealistic growth.

### REQUIRED OUTPUT

**A. Financial Executive Summary**

**E. Financial Scenarios**

**F. Break-even Analysis**

**G. Capital Requirements**

**I. Financial Risks**

**J. Assumption Register**

**K. Financial Conclusion**

Answer:
Is the business economically plausible, and which assumptions are most important to its financial viability?

### FINANCIAL RULES

- Never fabricate actual costs or market prices.
- Clearly label estimates.
- Show formulas and calculations.
- Use ranges when exact data is unavailable.
- Distinguish gross profit, operating profit and cash flow.
- Do not treat projected revenue as validated demand.
- Do not assume that LTV/CAC estimates are reliable when based on unverified retention or acquisition assumptions.
- Explain which real-world data is needed to improve the model.
