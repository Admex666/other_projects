### CENTRAL QUESTION

What is the smallest, most reliable and cost-effective experiment that can determine whether the critical business assumptions are true?

### TASKS

#### 1. Prioritize Critical Assumptions
Rank assumptions according to:
- Potential impact if false.
- Current uncertainty.
- Cost of being wrong.
- Ability to test.

Identify the assumptions that should be tested before product development.

#### 2. Identify Testable Hypotheses
Rewrite each important assumption as a clear, falsifiable hypothesis.

Example:

Weak:
"Customers will like the product."

Better:
"At least 5 out of 20 qualified target customers will agree to a paid pilot at the proposed price."

Use thresholds appropriate to the actual business.

#### 3. Generate Experiment Alternatives
Consider relevant methods:
- Customer interviews.
- Problem discovery interviews.
- Landing page tests.
- Smoke tests.
- Prototype tests.
- Preorders.
- Deposits.
- Paid pilots.
- Concierge MVP.
- Wizard-of-Oz tests.
- Pricing experiments.
- Letters of intent, where commercially meaningful.

Do not recommend every method automatically.

#### 4. Compare Experiments
Evaluate each experiment by:
- Cost.
- Duration.
- Required resources.
- Evidence quality.
- Practical difficulty.
- Risk of misleading results.
- Expected decision value.

#### 5. Select the Best Experiment
Recommend the experiment that offers the best balance between cost, reliability and decision value.

#### 6. Design the Experiment
Specify:
- Hypothesis.
- Target customer.
- Sample or participant selection.
- Recruitment method.
- Procedure.
- Materials required.
- Data to collect.
- Metrics.
- Success threshold.
- Failure threshold.
- Duration.
- Estimated cost.

#### 7. Prevent False Validation
Identify possible biases:
- Leading questions.
- Selection bias.
- Social desirability bias.
- Small or unrepresentative samples.
- Free-trial bias.
- Intentions mistaken for behavior.
- Founder confirmation bias.

#### 8. Define Decision Rules
Explain what happens if:
- The experiment succeeds.
- The experiment partially succeeds.
- The experiment fails.
- Results are inconclusive.

Map each outcome to the next decision state.

#### 9. Design the Learning Loop
Explain how results should update:
- The original hypothesis.
- The question graph.
- The assumption register.
- The evidence base.
- The executive decision.

### IMPORTANT RULES

- Prefer observed customer behavior over stated intentions.
- Prefer real payment or commitment over hypothetical purchase interest when appropriate.
- Do not confuse a prototype test with commercial validation.
- Do not recommend building a complete product to test a hypothesis that can be tested more cheaply.
- Do not invent experimental results.
- Define success and failure criteria before running the experiment.
- Consider the business model and customer purchasing process when selecting the experiment.
