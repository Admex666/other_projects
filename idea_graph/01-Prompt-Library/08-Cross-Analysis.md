### CENTRAL QUESTION

What does the combined evidence tell us about the attractiveness, viability and feasibility of this business opportunity?

### ANALYSIS TASKS

#### 1. Integrated Findings
Identify the most important conclusions across all research domains.

Do not simply repeat the reports.

Explain what they mean together.

#### 2. Cross-Domain Relationships
Identify:
- Dependencies.
- Influences.
- Feedback loops.
- Contradictions.
- Shared assumptions.
- Conflicting evidence.

Example:
A high customer acquisition cost may undermine an otherwise attractive market opportunity.

#### 3. Assumption Analysis
Identify the assumptions that multiple conclusions depend on.

Determine whether several positive findings are actually based on the same unverified assumption.

#### 4. Critical Success Factors
Determine what must be true for the business to succeed.

Rank these conditions by importance.

#### 5. Fatal Flaw Analysis
Identify conditions that could fundamentally invalidate the opportunity.

Distinguish:
- Fatal flaws.
- Serious but manageable risks.
- Ordinary execution challenges.

#### 6. Evidence Quality
Evaluate:
- Source reliability.
- Evidence relevance.
- Recency.
- Independence.
- Methodological limitations.
- Missing evidence.

Avoid treating several sources repeating the same original claim as independent confirmation.

#### 7. Opportunity Attractiveness
Assess separately:
- Market attractiveness.
- Customer demand strength.
- Competitive positioning.
- Business model viability.
- Financial potential.
- Execution feasibility.
- Growth potential.

Use a 1–5 rating only as a descriptive aid. Explain every rating and its limitations.

#### 8. Evidence Confidence
For each major conclusion assess confidence separately from attractiveness.

Use:
- High.
- Medium.
- Low.

Explain why.

#### 9. Value of Information
Determine which unanswered questions could most change the decision.

Rank them by:
- Potential decision impact.
- Current uncertainty.
- Cost of obtaining the answer.
- Expected usefulness of new information.

#### 10. Research Prioritization
Identify which additional research is genuinely necessary.

Do not recommend more research when a real customer experiment would provide stronger evidence.

### IMPORTANT RULES

- Do not produce a simplistic average score.
- Do not hide uncertainty.
- Do not overvalue positive findings.
- Do not treat estimates as verified facts.
- Do not confuse market attractiveness with actual customer validation.
- Do not make the final investment decision.
- Explicitly identify missing input documents.
