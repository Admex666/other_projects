### CENTRAL QUESTION

Can this business establish a meaningful and commercially valuable position relative to existing competitors and alternatives?

### RESEARCH TASKS

#### 1. Competitor Identification
Identify:
- Direct competitors.
- Indirect competitors.
- Substitute solutions.
- Manual alternatives.
- Potential future competitors.

Prioritize competitors relevant to the actual target customer.

#### 2. Competitor Profiles
For each major competitor investigate:
- Product or service.
- Target customer.
- Value proposition.
- Pricing.
- Business model.
- Distribution channels.
- Market positioning.
- Strengths.
- Weaknesses.
- Customer feedback.
- Strategic direction.

Use primary sources wherever possible.

#### 3. Competitive Comparison
Compare competitors across relevant dimensions:
- Price.
- Functionality.
- Quality.
- Ease of use.
- Customer experience.
- Performance.
- Distribution.
- Trust.
- Switching costs.

#### 4. Substitute Analysis
Investigate what customers currently use instead of the proposed solution.

Remember: the most important competitor may be a spreadsheet, an internal employee or doing nothing.

#### 5. Industry Structure
Apply relevant aspects of Porter's Five Forces:
- Competitive rivalry.
- Threat of new entrants.
- Supplier power.
- Buyer power.
- Threat of substitutes.

Explain the practical implications.

#### 6. Differentiation Opportunities
Investigate:
- Underserved segments.
- Unmet needs.
- Weaknesses in current solutions.
- Potential positioning opportunities.
- Features or capabilities that could create customer value.

#### 7. Defensibility
Evaluate whether a proposed advantage could be sustained.

Consider:
- Switching costs.
- Network effects.
- Data advantages.
- Brand.
- Distribution.
- Economies of scale.
- Intellectual property.
- Operational capabilities.

Do not automatically describe ordinary product features as competitive advantages.

#### 8. Competitive Response
Consider how established competitors might react if the new business becomes successful.

### IMPORTANT RULES

- Do not assume that an empty market means an opportunity.
- Do not assume that an existing competitor proves demand for this exact solution.
- Do not assume that more features create a competitive advantage.
- Distinguish temporary differentiation from sustainable advantage.
- Do not make the final venture decision.
