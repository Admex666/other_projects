### CENTRAL QUESTION

Can the proposed business create meaningful customer value and capture enough of that value to sustain an attractive business?

### TASKS

#### 1. Value Proposition
Define:
- Target customer.
- Customer problem.
- Proposed solution.
- Customer benefit.
- Differentiation.
- Reason to switch.

Evaluate whether the value proposition is specific, relevant and credible.

#### 2. Business Model Design
Analyze:
- Customer segments.
- Value propositions.
- Channels.
- Customer relationships.
- Revenue streams.
- Key resources.
- Key activities.
- Key partners.
- Cost structure.

Use Business Model Canvas and Lean Canvas selectively where useful.

#### 3. Revenue Model
Investigate suitable revenue mechanisms:
- Subscription.
- Transaction fees.
- Direct sales.
- Usage-based pricing.
- Licensing.
- Services.
- One-time purchases.
- Hybrid models.

Explain which models fit the customer behavior and market structure.

#### 4. Pricing Strategy
Assess:
- Value-based pricing.
- Competitor-based pricing.
- Cost-based pricing.
- Customer budget.
- Price sensitivity.
- Packaging and tiers.
- Potential expansion revenue.

Do not recommend an exact price without sufficient evidence.

#### 5. Go-to-Market
Develop a preliminary strategy:
- Initial customer segment.
- Positioning.
- Acquisition channels.
- Sales process.
- Conversion mechanism.
- Onboarding.
- Retention.

#### 6. Business Model Risks
Identify:
- Weak value capture.
- Excessive dependence on partners.
- High operational complexity.
- Low customer retention potential.
- Difficult distribution.
- Weak monetization.
- Misalignment between price and customer value.

#### 7. Alternative Models
Generate at least two plausible alternative business models where relevant.

Compare them by:
- Customer value.
- Revenue potential.
- Cost structure.
- Complexity.
- Scalability.
- Risk.

### REQUIRED OUTPUT

**A. Business Model Summary**

**B. Value Proposition**

**C. Business Model Canvas**

**D. Revenue Model Comparison**

**E. Pricing Hypotheses**

**F. Go-to-Market Hypothesis**

**G. Alternative Business Models**

**H. Business Model Risks**

**I. Critical Assumptions**

**J. Business Model Conclusion**

Answer:
Is there a coherent and commercially plausible mechanism for creating and capturing value?

### IMPORTANT RULES

- Do not confuse product desirability with business model viability.
- Do not assume that a technically useful product can automatically be monetized.
- Clearly distinguish validated elements from hypotheses.
- Do not invent customer willingness to pay.
- Connect recommendations to available customer and market evidence.
