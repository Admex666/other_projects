### CENTRAL QUESTION

Does a specific customer segment experience an important, insufficiently solved problem for which it may be willing to pay?

### RESEARCH TASKS

#### 1. Customer Identification
Identify:
- Main customer segments.
- Users.
- Buyers.
- Decision-makers.
- Influencers.
- Other relevant stakeholders.

Describe the most promising customer profiles.

#### 2. Problem Investigation
For each relevant segment investigate:
- What problem do they experience?
- How frequently does it occur?
- How severe is it?
- What consequences does it create?
- What financial or operational costs does it generate?
- What happens if they do nothing?

#### 3. Jobs To Be Done
Identify:
- Functional jobs.
- Emotional jobs.
- Social jobs.
- Desired outcomes.
- Context in which the customer experiences the problem.

#### 4. Current Alternatives
Investigate:
- Existing products.
- Manual processes.
- Internal solutions.
- Workarounds.
- Doing nothing.

Determine why customers choose these alternatives and what limitations they experience.

#### 5. Customer Dissatisfaction and Switching
Investigate:
- What makes customers dissatisfied?
- What prevents them from switching?
- What triggers a purchasing decision?
- What switching costs exist?
- What level of improvement would justify switching?

#### 6. Willingness to Pay
Investigate available evidence of:
- Existing spending on similar solutions.
- Customer budgets.
- Purchasing behavior.
- Price sensitivity.
- Buying authority.
- Procurement processes.
- Evidence of actual willingness to pay.

Distinguish stated willingness to pay from observed purchasing behavior.

#### 7. Customer Acquisition Context
Identify:
- Where potential customers can be reached.
- How they discover solutions.
- Which channels they trust.
- How long purchasing decisions may take.
- What objections they may have.

#### 8. Customer Segmentation and Prioritization
Rank segments by:
- Problem severity.
- Frequency.
- Economic impact.
- Purchasing power.
- Accessibility.
- Existing alternatives.
- Potential willingness to pay.

### EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Use:
- Customer interviews where available.
- Surveys with methodological limitations clearly stated.
- Industry research.
- Customer reviews.
- Public discussions and forums.
- Case studies.
- Purchasing and spending data.
- Competitor customer evidence.

Do not claim to have interviewed real customers unless actual interview data was provided.

Do not treat online comments as representative market statistics.

### IMPORTANT RULES

- Do not assume that people want a product because they complain about a problem.
- Do not assume that interest means purchase intent.
- Do not assume that purchase intent means actual payment.
- Explicitly distinguish observed behavior from stated preferences.
- Identify where real-world customer testing is required.
