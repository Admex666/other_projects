# PROMPT 01 — IDEA FRAMING (PEERPLAY)
## Transform a Business Idea into a Structured Opportunity

### ROLE

Act as a senior venture analyst and business model strategist.

Your task is to transform an initial business idea into a structured, neutral and researchable business opportunity.

You must not assume that the idea is viable, attractive or commercially feasible.

---

### INPUT

**Business idea:**
PeerPlay: A corporate organization intelligence and diagnostic system delivered through a hybrid team-building experience ("The Trading Game") and automated Organizational Network Analysis (ONA).

1. The Problem: Companies spend millions on traditional team buildings (go-kart, escape rooms, drinking), but on Monday, organizational silos, friction between departments (Finance vs. Sales vs. Manufacturing), and communication bottlenecks remain unchanged. Management and HR lack objective, behavioral data on how employees actually collaborate under pressure.
2. The Solution ("Trading Game"): A 90–120 minute intensive, gamified workshop. Physical + Digital hybrid format. Teams build geometric shapes from raw physical materials (paper, scissors, rulers) and sell them to a Central Bank for money. Initial resources are distributed unequally, forcing teams to negotiate, trade, and collaborate across departments to survive. Simultaneously, every participant holds a mobile companion WebApp to record transactions, loans, and post-game impressions with 1 tap.
3. The Value / Output: Immediate data-driven ONA diagnostic report for leadership:
   - Network Map (Sociogram): visualizes cross-departmental communication, revealing real collaboration gaps and isolated silos.
   - Informal Brokers: identifies key informal connectors across whom trust and information actually flow.
   - Psychometrics & Energy Tracking (Δ Energy): quantifies pre- and post-workshop mental energy levels and negotiation reliability.
4. Business Model & Positioning: Not sold as an off-the-shelf SaaS, but as a premium organizational development workshop + executive diagnostic report. Initially pilot format, later 400,000 – 600,000 HUF (~$1,100 – $1,650 EUR) per session.
5. Customer Promise: 90 minutes of engaging play for the team, but a real, decision-grade "X-ray" of organizational dynamics for executive leadership.

**Geography / target market:**
Hungary and Central-Eastern Europe (CEE) initially, mid-size to enterprise companies (50–500+ employees).

**Potential customers:**
HR Directors, Heads of People & Culture, COOs, Managing Directors / CEOs, and external Training / OD consultants as distribution partners.

**Founder capabilities and experience:**
Experienced in workshop facilitation, organizational design, software development for web applications.

**Available resources and budget:**
Bootstrapped prototype stage: web application prototype available, physical workshop mechanics designed, seeking commercial validation and repeatable pilot sales.

**Personal or business objectives:**
Validate willingness to pay for diagnostic team building; determine whether this can scale beyond founder facilitation into a scalable venture or licensed platform.

**Constraints:**
Limited sales bandwidth; personal facilitation limits rapid scaling; GDPR and employee privacy constraints regarding named sociograms.

---

### OBJECTIVE

Create the initial analytical foundation for the Venture Intelligence System.

### TASKS

1. Define the business idea in clear, neutral language.
2. Identify the potential customer segments.
3. Distinguish users, buyers, decision-makers and other stakeholders.
4. Describe the suspected customer problem.
5. Describe the proposed solution and its expected value.
6. Identify possible revenue models.
7. Classify the business model (e.g. SaaS, marketplace, service, e-commerce, physical product).
8. Identify the most relevant industry and market boundaries.
9. Identify the founder-dependent factors that could influence feasibility.
10. Separate known information, assumptions, estimates and unknowns.
11. Identify potential fatal flaws that deserve early investigation.
12. Determine which of the six analytical domains are relevant and whether additional specialized questions are needed.

### QUESTION GRAPH DESIGN

Create a hierarchical question graph.

Start with this central question:
"Is this a sufficiently attractive and feasible business opportunity worth pursuing?"

Break it down into the relevant main questions and subquestions.

For every question provide:
- Unique ID (Q-001, Q-002, etc.)
- Question
- Why it matters
- What evidence is required
- Suggested research method
- Related questions
- Dependencies
- Decision relevance

Identify:
- Independent questions that can be researched in parallel.
- Questions that depend on other findings.
- Questions that may create new branches.
- Questions that could invalidate the opportunity.

Use Mermaid flowchart syntax to visualize the initial question graph.

### REQUIRED OUTPUT

**A. Opportunity Definition**
A concise description of the business opportunity.

**B. Business Model Hypothesis**
Customer, problem, solution, value proposition, revenue model and delivery mechanism.

**C. Customer and Stakeholder Map**
Distinct breakdown of Buyers, End-Users, Facilitators, and Subjects.

**D. Assumption Register**
For each assumption:
- ID (A-001, A-002...)
- Description
- Category (Customer / Market / Solution / Competition / Business Model / Execution)
- Why it matters
- Impact if false
- Current confidence (Low / Medium / High)

**E. Specialized Analytical Questions**
Specific questions tailored to this unique hybrid physical-digital OD model.
