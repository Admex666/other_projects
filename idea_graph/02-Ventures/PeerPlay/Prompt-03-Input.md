# PROMPT 03 — CUSTOMER INTELLIGENCE (PEERPLAY)
## Customer Problem, Decision-Making and Willingness to Pay

### ROLE

Act as a senior customer discovery specialist, enterprise B2B sales researcher, and organizational behavior expert.

You have access to deep research and investigative synthesis capabilities. Conduct an evidence-based assessment of enterprise buyers, departmental silos, and willingness to pay.

---

### INPUT

**Business opportunity definition:**
PeerPlay: A corporate diagnostic workshop ("The Trading Game" simulation) + mobile WebApp companion + immediate Organizational Network Analysis (ONA) diagnostic report.
Core value proposition: An engaging 90–120 min team experience for employees that simultaneously delivers an objective, data-driven "X-ray" to executive management showing real communication silos, informal brokers, and collaboration bottlenecks.
Proposed pricing: 400,000 – 600,000 HUF (~$1,100 – $1,650 EUR) per session.
Target segment: 150–500 employee mid-market companies and enterprises in Hungary / CEE.

**Relevant research questions from Question Graph:**
- Q-101: Which organizational collaboration problems (silos, inter-departmental conflicts, post-restructuring misalignment) are painful enough to trigger a corporate purchase?
- Q-102: Who is the true economic buyer (HR Director vs. COO vs. Business Unit Head vs. CEO)? Who owns the budget, and who has veto power?
- Q-103: How do organizations currently diagnose or attempt to fix cross-departmental friction (e.g., traditional team-buildings, engagement surveys, 360 reviews, external OD consultants)? What are the documented shortcomings of these alternatives?
- Q-104: How frequently does this pain occur, and what are the specific triggers that make a company look for a solution?
- Q-105: Are corporate decision-makers genuinely willing to pay a premium for diagnostic data, or do they primarily buy team building for entertainment and morale?
- Q-106: What specific proof, metrics, or validation would convince a skeptical HR Director or CEO to sign a pilot contract?
- Q-703 & Q-704: How do employees and management perceive behavioral data collection? Does fear of workplace surveillance (or distrust of ONA reporting) create adoption resistance?

---

### CENTRAL QUESTION

Is there a genuine, urgent, and monetizable customer problem regarding organizational silos and collaboration, and is management willing to pay 400,000 – 600,000 HUF for a hybrid diagnostic workshop rather than traditional alternatives?

---

### RESEARCH TASKS

#### 1. Problem Severity & Urgent Triggers
- Investigate the tangible business cost of organizational silos and cross-functional friction (e.g., delayed product launches, conflicting KPIs, employee turnover, missed sales).
- Identify the specific corporate events/triggers that create urgency (e.g., M&A / post-merger integration, rapid scaling, new leadership, cross-departmental escalations).
- Determine whether this problem is treated as a "hair-on-fire" urgency or a passive, chronic background nuisance.

#### 2. Buyer Persona & Stakeholder Dynamics
- Profile the primary Economic Buyer:
  - HR Director / Chief People Officer (Focus: employee engagement, culture, development, compliance).
  - Chief Operating Officer / Operations Director (Focus: workflow efficiency, cross-department handoffs, delivery speed).
  - CEO / Managing Director (Focus: strategic alignment, revenue impact, breaking silos).
- Map internal stakeholder conflict: What management wants (objective visibility into who collaborates with whom) vs. what employees fear (surveillance, performance labeling, micromanagement).

#### 3. Current Alternatives & Discontent
- Analyze how companies currently spend their team-building and OD budgets:
  - Experiential entertainment (go-kart, escape rooms, dinners, sports events).
  - Standard training / facilitation (soft-skill workshops, communication training).
  - Passive HR analytics (annual Gallup/engagement surveys, 360-degree reviews).
- Document why buyers are dissatisfied with existing team buildings ("fun on Friday, but silos remain on Monday") and where the gap exists.

#### 4. Willingness to Pay & Budget Mechanics
- Identify which corporate budget code would typically fund PeerPlay:
  - Team Building / Event Budget (often managed by HR or Office Management, low scrutiny, but expects entertainment).
  - L&D / Training Budget (requires learning outcomes, personal skill development).
  - Organizational Development (OD) / Change Management Budget (strategic, higher ticket size, but harder to access).
  - Executive / Operational Discretionary Budget (owned by CEO/COO).
- Assess price sensitivity: Is 400,000 – 600,000 HUF considered high, low, or standard for a 90–120 minute workshop in Hungary and CEE?

#### 5. Objections, Ethical Barriers & Pilot Requirements
- Detail the top objections a B2B sales pitch will encounter:
  - "Will our employees feel they are being spied on?" (Surveillance stigma).
  - "Is a 90-minute game really representative of our daily work?" (Ecological validity skepticism).
  - "What do we do with this report on Monday?" (Actionability deficit).
- Formulate the exact success criteria and proof points needed to secure an initial paid pilot.

---

### REQUIRED OUTPUT STRUCTURE

Please format your analysis according to the following structure:

**1. Executive Findings** (Core verdict on customer pain, willingness to pay, and stakeholder alignment).

**2. The Anatomy of Corporate Silos** (Tangible costs, chronic friction points, and buying triggers).

**3. Detailed Stakeholder & Buyer Persona Analysis**
- Economic Buyer vs. Champion vs. User vs. Blocker.
- Buyer incentives, KPI alignment, and veto points.

**4. Alternative Comparison & The Satisfaction Gap**
- Direct comparison table: Traditional Team Building vs. Engagement Surveys vs. Pure ONA Software vs. PeerPlay.

**5. Budget Allocation & Willingness-to-Pay Analysis**
- Target budget category breakdown.
- Evaluation of the 400,000 – 600,000 HUF price point against corporate purchasing thresholds.

**6. Employee Trust & Ethical/Surveillance Friction**
- Practical risks of workplace pushback and strategies to frame ONA constructively.

**7. Sales Pitch Objections & Pilot Conversion Strategy**
- Top 5 buyer objections and recommended counter-evidence.
- The minimum viable proof required to close a paid pilot.

**8. Evidence Log & Sources** (B2B HR research, case studies, industry benchmarks).
