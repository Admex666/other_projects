### FINANCIAL MODELING TASKS

#### 1. Unit Economics & Contribution Margin Breakdown
Calculate the per-session economics at three price points: 400,000 HUF, 500,000 HUF, and 600,000 HUF.
- **Direct Variable Costs (COGS):**
  - Physical materials per session (paper, stationery, game tokens).
  - External / Contractor Facilitator fees (benchmark market rates for freelance senior and junior trainers in Hungary/CEE).
  - Cloud server & WebApp compute/hosting costs per session.
  - Travel and local logistics.
- Calculate:
  - **Gross Profit (HUF & EUR)**.
  - **Gross Margin (%)**.
  - **Contribution Margin (%)**.

#### 2. Fixed Cost Structure (OpEx)
Model the operational overhead for Years 1, 2, and 3:
- Core team / founder compensation or draw.
- Ongoing software development & maintenance (WebApp bug fixes, ONA graph rendering enhancements).
- Marketing & B2B sales expenses (events, content, CRM, outbound tools, travel).
- Administrative, legal, and accounting expenses (GDPR compliance, insurance, invoicing).

#### 3. Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) & LTV Modeling
- **Direct B2B Outbound/Inbound CAC:** Estimate total sales/marketing expense divided by new clients acquired in Year 1 and Year 2.
- **Partner Channel CAC:** Blended cost when paying a 20–30% reseller commission.
- **Customer Lifetime Value (LTV):**
  - Scenario 1: Pure 1-off (1.0 sessions per customer lifetime).
  - Scenario 2: Modest re-engagement (1.4–1.8 sessions per client over 24 months).
  - Scenario 3: Recurring annual client (2.5–3.5 sessions across multiple departments).
- Compute **LTV / CAC ratio** and **CAC payback period (months)**.

#### 4. Break-Even & Sensitivity Analysis
- Calculate the exact break-even point in terms of monthly and annual workshop volume.
- Sensitivity Matrix: Stress-test profitability against:
  - Drop in session price to 350,000 HUF.
  - Higher facilitator fees (+30%).
  - Higher CAC due to corporate procurement resistance.

#### 5. Working Capital & Cash Flow Timing
- Account for CEE corporate payment delays (average 30–60 days, risk of 90 days in large enterprises).
- How much working capital buffer is required in Year 1 to survive payment lag?
